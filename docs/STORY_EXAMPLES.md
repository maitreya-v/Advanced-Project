# Directed Path Story Examples

## Current character-led implementation

The original examples below document the earlier explanatory style. The user subsequently approved a slower fictional-character story: Mira loves writing about a fox who steals the moon, and an unfinished story gives her family a concrete concern to discuss. The implemented **Path story** workspace uses this character continuity, isolates the chosen route and traces one relationship per chapter. Graph facts appear in a separate source drawer. Optional local Qwen generation writes a new story for the selected route; the authored character version remains available offline.

Fictional scenes and dialogue are not observations from the dataset. No patient treatment response is invented as evidence.

Read the [actual local-Qwen story](GENERATED_CHARACTER_STORY.md) or open its [playable standalone export](character-story-demo.html). This example was generated by the implemented pipeline rather than written as a hypothetical output in chat.

These examples use the actual 2026 age-8 graph in the combined repository. Node names, edge directions, signs and strengths were checked against its source records. The prose below is an illustrative specification example written during analysis, not output from a newly run Qwen session.

## 1. What the route options will look like

Selected start: **Diagnosis Status**. Selected end: **Quality of Life**. Maximum route length: **five directed edges**.

There are **eight simple directed routes within this bound**. They are listed here in increasing hop count, with deterministic node-name ordering within equal lengths. Longer routes may exist outside this bound.

| Option | Title | Hops | Full route |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | The direct summary connection | 1 | Diagnosis Status → Quality of Life |
| 2 | Formal school support | 4 | Diagnosis Status → School Support Plan → Learning Accommodations → Functional Impairment → Quality of Life |
| 3 | Access to behavioral therapy | 4 | Diagnosis Status → Treatment Access → Behavioral Therapy → Functional Impairment → Quality of Life |
| 4 | Treatment and side effects | 4 | Diagnosis Status → Treatment Access → Medication Treatment → Treatment Side Effects → Quality of Life |
| 5 | Access to school accommodations | 4 | Diagnosis Status → Treatment Access → School Accommodations → Functional Impairment → Quality of Life |
| 6 | Behavioral therapy through symptoms | 5 | Diagnosis Status → Treatment Access → Behavioral Therapy → Symptom Severity → Functional Impairment → Quality of Life |
| 7 | Digital support through symptoms | 5 | Diagnosis Status → Treatment Access → Digital Therapeutics → Symptom Severity → Functional Impairment → Quality of Life |
| 8 | Medication through symptoms | 5 | Diagnosis Status → Treatment Access → Medication Treatment → Symptom Severity → Functional Impairment → Quality of Life |

The route titles summarize model structure. They do not recommend an intervention. Option 1 is a valid direct edge and should remain available, although it provides less intermediate detail. Options 2 and 5 must stay distinct: School Support Plan and School Accommodations are separate nodes in the source.

Selecting an option should highlight its route immediately. Generating a story is a separate action.

## 2. Full example: From recognition to everyday life

Selected option: **8 — Medication through symptoms**.

```text
START: Diagnosis Status
  → Treatment Access
  → Medication Treatment
  → Symptom Severity
  → Functional Impairment
  → Quality of Life :END
```

### Opening — A route with several steps

“A diagnosis is the starting point of this story, but the route we have selected reaches quality of life through several intermediate concepts. In the 2026 graph for age 8, it passes through access to treatment, medication, symptoms and everyday impairment. Let's follow each arrow to see what the model is representing.”

**On screen:** show the complete graph faintly, mark Diagnosis Status as Start and Quality of Life as End, and display the five-edge route as a preview. Nothing has been traversed yet.

### Step 1 — Recognition connects to access

“The first arrow connects Diagnosis Status to Treatment Access. The model describes diagnosis as opening a structured treatment pathway. Access is an explicit step in this route: the story does not jump directly from recognition to a treatment outcome.”

**On screen:** trace Diagnosis Status → Treatment Access and illuminate Treatment Access. Show step 1 of 5; retain the full breadcrumb.

### Step 2 — One branch leads to medication

“From Treatment Access, this route continues to Medication Treatment. This is the medication branch we chose from the route list. The graph also contains other branches, but this story will stay with the selected sequence so we can understand it from beginning to end.”

**On screen:** trace Treatment Access → Medication Treatment. Keep the first edge illuminated as part of the visited route. Do not highlight alternative branches as though they belong to this path.

### Step 3 — Symptoms enter the explanation

“The next arrow runs from Medication Treatment to Symptom Severity and carries a negative sign. In the model's explanation, medication can reduce symptom burden. Here, a negative sign describes a reduction in the target concept; it does not mean that the story has taken an unfavorable turn.”

**On screen:** trace Medication Treatment → Symptom Severity in the negative-sign color. Show the source explanation in the evidence drawer.

### Step 4 — Symptoms connect to everyday impairment

“Symptom Severity then connects to Functional Impairment with a positive sign. The model represents more severe symptoms as contributing to greater impairment. This step connects the symptom description to the difficulties represented in everyday functioning.”

**On screen:** trace Symptom Severity → Functional Impairment. The first four edges are now visited; only the final segment remains ahead.

### Step 5 — The route reaches quality of life

“The final arrow connects Functional Impairment to Quality of Life with a negative sign. The model describes impairment as reducing quality of life. We have reached the endpoint, and the complete route now shows how access, treatment, symptoms and impairment connect within this authored explanation.”

**On screen:** trace Functional Impairment → Quality of Life, mark End as reached and show all five directed edges together.

### Recap — What this route helps us see

“The value of this route is its intermediate detail. Rather than stopping at a direct diagnosis-to-quality-of-life link, it shows the concepts the model places between them. It is one of several represented explanations, not a forecast for an individual child or a measured causal effect of the complete chain.”

**On screen:** offer Replay, Inspect relationships, Compare with school support, and Choose another route. Keep the complete route visible.

### Checked relationship data behind this story

| Step | Directed relationship | Sign | Authored strength |
|---:|---|:---:|---:|
| 1 | Diagnosis Status → Treatment Access | + | 0.88 |
| 2 | Treatment Access → Medication Treatment | + | 0.88 |
| 3 | Medication Treatment → Symptom Severity | − | 0.78 |
| 4 | Symptom Severity → Functional Impairment | + | 0.84 |
| 5 | Functional Impairment → Quality of Life | − | 0.86 |

These are model inputs, not probabilities or measured effects. The 2026 snapshot has no matching snapshot-specific review report. Some relationships have contextual literature in the local story registry; that context does not validate all five edges as a complete mediated effect.

## 3. Alternative story: A route through school support

Selected option: **2 — Formal school support**.

> “This time, the story moves from Diagnosis Status to School Support Plan. The model connects recognition with formal support planning, then takes us to Learning Accommodations: a step from the plan to the support represented in the learning environment. The next arrow points negatively toward Functional Impairment, describing accommodations as reducing school-related difficulties. The final arrow links impairment negatively with Quality of Life. This route gives us an explanation centered on school support, with a different set of intermediate concepts from the medication route.”

Trace sequence:

1. Diagnosis Status → School Support Plan: +, 0.84.
2. School Support Plan → Learning Accommodations: +, 0.82.
3. Learning Accommodations → Functional Impairment: −, 0.42.
4. Functional Impairment → Quality of Life: −, 0.86.

Both routes share their start and end. They should not be collapsed into one story: their explanatory focus differs. Do not compare their products of weights to conclude which is more effective.

## 4. A balancing story: Following the side-effect branch

Selected option: **4 — Treatment and side effects**.

> “The first two steps are familiar: Diagnosis Status connects to Treatment Access, which connects to Medication Treatment. But this selected route now follows a different branch, toward Treatment Side Effects. The model includes side effects as a positive connection from medication and then gives side effects a negative connection to Quality of Life. This story brings a represented tradeoff into view. It does not cancel the separate symptom-focused route, and the graph does not calculate how these routes combine for a particular person.”

Trace sequence:

1. Diagnosis Status → Treatment Access: +, 0.88.
2. Treatment Access → Medication Treatment: +, 0.88.
3. Medication Treatment → Treatment Side Effects: +, 0.52.
4. Treatment Side Effects → Quality of Life: −, 0.18.

This option matters because an engaging storyteller should not select only favorable chains. The interface should let the viewer inspect competing or complementary represented explanations.

## 5. A shorter, human-centered presentation

For a classroom demonstration, the same medication route can be introduced more simply:

> “Recognition is only the beginning of this graph's story. From Diagnosis Status, we follow the arrow to Treatment Access, then to Medication Treatment. The route next brings symptoms into view, connects those symptoms to everyday impairment, and finally reaches Quality of Life. Each arrow adds a piece of the explanation. Choosing a school-support route would tell a different story between the same beginning and ending.”

This is an explanatory narrative rather than an invented patient outcome. A future fictional vignette could add a named character and everyday scene, but invented details should be explicitly identified and should not imply a clinical result absent from the data.

## 6. Recommended first demonstration

1. Show all eight options and preview their arrows.
2. Play the full five-hop medication story.
3. Return to the list and select school support.
4. Show the shared start and end, then the different intermediate nodes.
5. Select the side-effect route to demonstrate balanced exploration.
6. Open a relationship's evidence panel and explain what is authored versus reviewed.

The demonstration should make the core idea visible: **same endpoints, different directed routes, different coherent stories**.
